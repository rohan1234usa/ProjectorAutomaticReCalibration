The algorithm

How the check will decide

This is the planned detector from the project brief (CLAUDE.md §4), step by step, most trusted first. It is designed first and built phase by phase; the build order's next phase is , . The pictures are real simulator frames. Where a figure computes something the detector will compute, it is a teaching illustration, made with the simulator's ground truth at hand, and it is labelled as one.

Step 0

Right after calibration is the truth

Calibration is the one moment the projectors are known to be aligned. Everything the camera measures in the next ten minutes becomes the baseline that later checks compare with: each projector's outline, the overlap's brightness, the edge positions. A new calibration replaces the baseline and resets the answer to NO.

Technical details

The trusted window is TRUSTED_WINDOW_S = s. In it the boxes are refined as more dark and lit frames arrive, and the pools for the overlap check are filled. The baseline stores the camera homography and marker centres, both boxes with corner covariance, the overlap geometry, per-piece edge positions, per-block brightness ratios, border steps, the lamp ratio, a noise model and (in reference mode) kernel offsets. No scenario moves a projector inside it.

Step 1

From camera pixels to millimetres on the screen

The camera sees the screen at a slant, so its pixels don't map evenly onto the screen. Four or more printed markers on the bezel are enough to work out the mapping and redraw the frame on an even millimetre grid. On that grid each projector's light is the four-sided shape the calibration placed. From here on every distance is in millimetres.

Illustration. This frame was redrawn with the camera position the simulator knows. The detector will work it out from the marker centres (phase 3).
Technical details
  • The homography is solved from marker centres, not corners: detected corners sit about 0.65 px inside the true ones (0.27 px with sub-pixel refinement), and centres cancel that. Straight-line fits to each marker's four outer edges locate a centre to p95 0.09 px in one frame (findings, 2026-10-07).
  • The canvas is CANVAS_PX_PER_MM = px per mm (shown here at 1). Frames are linear: the pedestal is subtracted and the gain divided, both known camera settings.
  • If the markers move more than FIDUCIAL_MOVE_PX = px, the camera moved: the mapping is solved again, the pools are reset, and a camera_moved warning is raised. A camera bump can never produce a YES.
Step 2 · primary

Boundary analysis: watch each projector's outline

Each projector lights a slightly tilted four-sided shape: its box. The outer edges of the combined picture always show against the unlit screen. The inner edges, hidden under the other projector, show in dark frames, through each projector's black level. Every 30 seconds the detector re-measures whatever edges it can see and fits how B has moved relative to A. This works whatever the content, which is why it is trusted first.

When the software hides the overlap's glow

Some blending software lifts each projector's black outside the overlap, so black looks even everywhere. Then the inner edges vanish in dark frames while aligned. After a drift, the lift no longer meets the other raster's edge and a step appears.

Technical details
  • Evidence comes as edge pieces of EDGE_PIECE_MM = mm. Each owns one projector, knows its outward normal, and is seen by content border, black level or blend ramp. It gives one number, n̂·u, with an uncertainty from its edge fit. Pieces below MIN_EDGE_SNR = are not used.
  • A and B are fitted jointly, with a shared camera-motion term, by weighted least squares with Huber weights. The model is the relative homography B-vs-A: 6 parameters first, 8 only if a pattern remains. A box needs evidence at two or more angles, or the interval answers "boundary not visible" (None), never a guess.
  • offset_mm is the largest displacement over the calibrated overlap. Planning budget: a black-level piece localizes to ≈ 0.11 mm in one frame and ≈ 0.02 mm over 30; a content-border piece to ≈ 0.004 mm.

Step 3 · secondary, triggered

The overlap check: three signs of misalignment

When the boundary reports an offset over TRIGGER_MM ( mm), and regardless every , the detector looks inside the overlap. It has pooled frames by kind. Textured patches go to the double-contour test, flat ones to the brightness test. Frames with motion, cuts or clipping are skipped.

1 · Double contours

When B has moved, everything in the overlap appears twice: once where A puts it, once where B does. A cepstrum, the Fourier transform of the log of the spectrum, turns "the picture plus a shifted copy" into a single peak at the shift. Text has its own repeats, such as line spacing and letter pitch. So the same is computed just outside the overlap, where only one projector shines, and subtracted: a pattern found on both sides is content, and a pattern found only inside is misalignment.

On real frames: core tiles minus control tiles

Illustration, not the detector. Log spectra are pooled over all core tiles and slides shown after B moved, then scored once. The detector will score each tile and ask the tiles to agree on one smooth motion (phase 6a).
Technical details
  • Tiles of TILE_MM = mm are cut on the camera grid, px at camera px per mm. Resampling first would lock peaks to the camera pitch. Core tiles have both blend weights ≥ CORE_MIN_WEIGHT = . Control tiles are found by walking along the blend gradient to CTRL_MARGIN_MM = mm beyond the far inner edge.
  • Per tile: a linear high-pass (the tile minus its blur), a Hann window, the log power spectrum, pooled. The cepstrum is the magnitude of its inverse transform, as a robust z-score (median, 1.4826·MAD). diff = z(core) − mean z(controls).
  • A disk of the floor, max(ECHO_FLOOR_CAMERA_PX, MIN_OFFSET_MM at this scale) = px ( mm), is blanked. The best peak outside it must reach MIN_PEAK_SNR = and NULL_FACTOR = × the control-vs-control null.
  • Verdicts here: resolved means the peak passes and is a peak of its own outside the floor. Unresolved means it passes but is only the flank of something higher inside the floor: an echo too close to the origin to size (the illustration's own rule). None means nothing passes, which is reported as None with an upper bound at the floor, never as 0.

2 · Brightness hotspots

Inside the overlap, A's fade-out and B's fade-in add up to exactly one. Move B and its fade moves with it, so the two no longer add to one: the overlap dims or brightens in a band shaped like the fade's slope. A dimmer lamp does something different: it tilts the whole overlap toward B's side. Flat content shows both.

3 · Offset borders

Where the picture's own edge runs through the overlap, A and B each draw a copy of it. Aligned, the copies coincide: one sharp step. Move B along the edge's normal and the step splits in two, each half as tall.

Reference mode

When the video feed can be read too

If the script can also read the picture being sent to the projectors (from a screen capture or an HDMI splitter), it no longer has to guess the content. For each patch it can work out where each projector put the content, comparing the sent picture with what the camera saw a moment later. That has no resolution floor.

Technical details

The last SOURCE_RING = source frames are kept. The one matching the camera frame best (NCC on downsampled images, at least MATCH_MIN_NCC = ) is used. Per tile, the cross spectrum Σ O·conj(S) and the power Σ|S|² are pooled into a Wiener kernel, k = IFFT(Σ O·conj(S) / (Σ|S|² + λ·mean power)), with λ = WIENER_LAMBDA = . The kernel of a control tile peaks where that projector alone puts the content. Offset = centroid(k_B) − centroid(k_A), so camera motion cancels. Planning estimate: within ≈ 0.1 mm for 0.5–4 mm offsets. Only repetitive content at whole-period shifts fools it, and then only the boundary can tell.

Step 4

Fusion and decision

The boundary's estimate leads. When the overlap check also ran and agrees, confidence rises. When it sees more, with two or more signs agreeing, it takes over. When the boundary sees much more than the overlap check, the detector suspects the edge fit and holds for an interval. The answer flips to YES only after of the last intervals are over tolerance. It returns to NO only when the offset falls below half the tolerance, and "can't tell" keeps the previous answer.

Technical details

Layers agree within AGREE_MM = mm. A disagreement may hold for at most HOLD_MAX_INTERVALS = . The overlap check contradicts the boundary only when it was informative: it resolved tiles, and the boundary offset is above the echo floor plus AGREE_MM. Each interval logs answer, offset_mm, offset_px, confidence, boundary_available, symptoms, mode and warnings as JSONL. Comparing those logs with the professor's own button presses is how the thresholds get set.

Self-referencing

What must never cause a YES

Each nuisance changes the picture without moving one projector relative to the other. The design answers each one by comparing A with B, never with an old picture. You can see each one on the sample data page.

NuisanceWhat it does to the pictureWhy it cannot fake a YES
Camera bumpEverything moves, markers includedMarkers re-solve the view; a shared camera term in the joint fit absorbs what is left; camera_moved resets the pools
Lamp dimmingOne projector's light drops (15% in the test)Fitted as a lamp gain that tilts the overlap rather than shifting the ramp; |g − 1| over LAMP_WARN is a warning
Room lightEverything brightens at onceComparisons are ratios between A and B and with flanks, and the room adds to both
Someone walking pastPart of the screen and a marker disappearThe frame difference exceeds MOTION_LEVEL, so the frame is skipped; markers hidden for long → can't tell
FlickerRolling bands, moving frame to frameExposure locked to a multiple of the refresh period; bands that move are not edges

The threshold

How big is "noticeable"?

YES means a person would notice. As a starting point, that is about one arcminute of the viewer's sight from the closest seat: D·tan(1′). It will later be learned from when the professor actually presses recalibrate. In the simulator it is swept.

Where the design comes from

From a vertical seam to any overlap

The design started as pseudocode dated 1 October 2026, written for two projectors side by side with a vertical blend band and a camera on that band. Its tests carry over: the echo, the seam profile, the reference kernel, the decision rule, the edge cases. The brief generalizes it in three ways:

Any arrangement

Side by side, stacked, rotated, at a corner, at different sizes, or mostly on top of each other. The overlap is measured as the intersection of two boxes.

The whole screen in view

The camera sees both projectors' full outlines and the markers at the corners, not just the band.

Edges first

Boundary analysis of each projector's box is the primary tool because it works whatever the content is. The overlap check confirms it.
