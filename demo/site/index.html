Two projectors, one picture

Have the projectors drifted apart?

Two projectors share one screen in a lecture hall, blended where they overlap so they read as a single picture. Over hours they drift. Today a professor checks by eye and presses the calibration software's recalibrate button. This project builds a camera-based check that answers the same question every : YES, they have drifted far enough for someone in the room to notice, or NO.

No hardware exists yet, so everything is developed against a simulator that renders the projectors, the screen, the room and the camera, and knows the true answer for every frame.

What drift looks like

Same frame, before and after projector B moved pixels

This is a patch of the overlap, where both projectors light the same part of the screen, as the simulated camera records it. Drag the slider. On the left the projectors are aligned and the text is crisp. On the right projector B has moved of its pixels ( mm) and every letter appears twice.

At 1 or 2 pixels the doubling is a slight blur. The check has to notice it at about the size a viewer would, and never mistake anything else for it.

How the check will work

Four ideas, in order of trust

The camera only ever sees the combined picture, whatever the lecture is showing. No test pattern is projected. The detector is designed first and built phase by phase; the algorithm page walks through it.

1

Watch each projector's edges

Each projector's light covers a slightly tilted rectangle on the screen. Its outline shows faintly even in black frames, because a projector's black is never fully black. Measure both outlines after calibration, then re-measure them every .

Boundary analysis →
2

Look inside the overlap

When the edges hint at a move, look at the overlap itself for doubled contours, for a band that is brighter or darker than at calibration, and for the picture's border splitting into two.

Overlap check →
3

Compare A with B, never with the past picture

Every measurement compares projector A with projector B. A bumped camera moves both, and a dimmer lamp changes brightness without moving anything, so neither can fake a YES.

What must never cause YES →
4

Decide carefully

Say YES only after of checks agree. Go back to NO only well below the threshold. Answer "can't tell" rather than guess; that keeps the previous answer.
